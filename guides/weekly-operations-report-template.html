Operations reporting

A weekly operations report that actually helps you decide what to fix

The point of a weekly report is not to create paperwork. It is to make movement, bottlenecks, and repeated manual work visible enough to act on.

Track six things

  1. What moved
  2. What consumed time
  3. Automation candidates
  4. Problems or failures
  5. Next-week priorities
  6. One process to simplify

Keep the metrics tied to action

For a revenue workflow, that might mean new leads, qualified leads, replies, calls, quotes, deals, and collected revenue. For another team, use the few metrics that describe the actual flow of work.

Turn repeated pain into a candidate queue

If the same task appears every week under “what consumed time,” document its trigger, inputs, desired output, biggest exception, and human approval point. That becomes your automation backlog.
